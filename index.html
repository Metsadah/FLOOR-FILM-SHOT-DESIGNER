A free canvas for visual thinking — collect stills, colors, sounds and references.

Getting things on the board

Drag tiles from the Board library, drop image files straight onto the canvas, or copy any picture (ShotDeck, Frameset, a browser tab) and paste with Cmd/Ctrl+V. Images take a caption via the selection bar. Links title themselves after their URL and fetch video previews automatically. Audio cards play with a tap on ▸. Files (PDFs get a first-page preview) travel with the project.

Working the canvas

Scroll to zoom, drag empty space to pan, F zooms to fit. The marquee tool M drags a box around several objects — then drag any one of them to move the whole group (duplicate/delete work on the group too). Double-click notes, text, to-do items and table cells to edit in place — Tab/Enter flow through table cells, Enter chains new to-do items. Tables grow like a spreadsheet: drag the corner handle to add or remove rows and columns. Drag anything onto the trash can at the bottom to delete it (undo works). Only photos rotate here; everything else stays straight.

Script and storyboard side by side — write left, break down right.

Script blocks

Drop a Film script or two-column AV script block from the library and double-click to write (left half = VIDEO, right half = AUDIO on AV blocks). Import… in the selection bar accepts .txt, .fountain, Final Draft .fdx and .pdf.

Break down

Select a script block and hit Break down: FLOOR detects scenes (INT./EXT. headings on film scripts, paragraphs on AV), creates a scene board per scene with the cast placed on it, and stacks linked storyboard rows next to your script — title | reference image | shot description. Open scene ↗ jumps to that board in the Shot designer, where every camera maps to one shot.

Select a storyboard row and hit the + chip below it (or "+ Row below" in the selection bar) to chain the next shot idea of that scene — everything underneath moves down automatically, so later scenes never get overlapped.

All the moodboard tools (notes, images, to-dos, tables, files, audio, paste, trash) work here too.

The production board — everything the shoot day needs, spatially arranged.

Production cards

The Day header card is the call-time block of a call sheet: pick the date in its selection bar, click the big general call (plus shooting call and est. wrap) to type times, and hit Sun from location ↻ — sunrise and sunset compute from the Location card's place. Production info and Location are field cards — label:value rows, click a value to edit, Tab hops fields, empty fields show light placeholders. Production info is a live window onto the production itself; each Location card holds one location (address, parking, power, nearest hospital, notes). Checklist drops a real to-do list born from a template — camera dept, grip & light, location, wrap, or blank. Call sheet and Day schedule are editable note templates. The Weather (live) card fetches a real forecast: give it a place and a date in its selection bar and hit Fetch ↻ (forecasts reach ~16 days ahead, free GFS-backed data from Open-Meteo).

Crew, cast & client — the People registry

The Crew, Cast and Client cards are live views of one per-production People registry: add a person on any card and they exist everywhere; edit a phone number once and every card updates. Click a cell to edit, Tab/Enter flow through the fields (Enter on the last row chains a new person), the + chip adds a row, the × chips remove one, and dragging a row's dotted grip reorders it. Paste list… (selection bar) imports a whole contact list: one person per line, FLOOR picks out name / role / phone / email / call time and shows a preview before adding. The registry will feed the call-sheet generator next phase. Drop map screenshots or location photos on the board; Export turns the whole board into a one-page PDF you can send as a call sheet today.

How to use FLOOR

Sketch the set, drop in camera and cast, block the movement — all by direct manipulation. 1 unit = 1 cm, grid dots are 50 cm apart.

Build the set

Wall W — drag to draw; snaps to 90° / 45° and to wall ends (Shift = free angle). Room R drags out four walls at once. Doors & windows: pick the tool, click a wall, drag the opening along it; select it for width and door-swing flip.

Objects

Drag anything from the library onto the canvas. Drag to move (Shift = grid snap), amber handle rotates, corner handle resizes, double-click to name. Props use real-world sizes. Cast comes in recognisable roles — protagonist (solid circle), antagonist (diamond), extra (open ring), child — plus animals (dog, cat, horse, or a generic one you can resize into anything). Custom props: rectangle, circle — or draw your own outline click by click (double-click or Enter closes it). Windows can get a curtain via their selection bar.

Cameras & lenses

Standard, gimbal, GoPro / crash cam and drone — switchable on any placed camera. Purple handles set field of view and reach in one drag, or pick a lens preset (16–135 mm full frame) in the selection bar.

Movement, dolly track & jib

Cameras, cast, vehicles and grip / lights can move: → Add movement, then keep extending with + Move point. Every path point is a keyframe with its own ghost: a rotate handle for facing / look direction, and on cameras its own FOV handles — framing can change along the move. Toggle curved / straight per path. Jib and technocrane keyframes work around the base: "+ Arm keyframe" adds a ghost with its own base point and a purple head handle, so the arm can swing and extend between positions — even while the base stays put. Hit ▶ (or P) to play all movement in the shot as a looping animation.

Dolly track (Grip & light) is laid as a curve of track points. Drop a camera near either end and it snaps on, taking the whole track as its movement path. The jib arm and technocrane have a purple head handle — drag it to swing and extend the arm around a fixed base. Drop a camera on the head to mount it: it rides the arm and keeps its aim relative to it, so swinging the arm pans the shot. Drop the crane's base anywhere on a dolly track and it clips on: from then on, dragging it slides the base along the rails (the arm stays adjustable), and pulling it well clear — or "Release from track" — unclips it.

Sun & time of day

The ☀ button drops a sun indicator on the shot. Drag the amber handle around the disc to set where the sun stands — the time of day follows (or type a time in the selection bar). Arrows show the light direction across the set. The N marker has its own handle: drag it to rotate north to match your location's real orientation.

Shot info & stills

The Shot info panel holds the shoot name, scene number and description, the script, plus date, start time, duration (10-minute steps) and preferred weather. When you add a new shot, date / duration / weather carry over and the start time continues where the previous shot wraps. Walls can be locked via the selection bar so you can't nudge your set by accident. Corners stay glued: dragging a wall pulls connected endpoints along, and the selection bar can switch to "drag moves all N walls" to slide a whole structure at once. Drag a wall's middle handle to bow it into a curve (drag back to the center to straighten). The gap tool cuts an open passage in any wall (select the gap to widen or delete it). Cameras take a one-line description and a Frame… image — a director's-viewfinder still pinned beside the camera that you can drag anywhere; both travel into the PDF. Annotate: the pencil tool draws freehand ink (lovely with an Apple Pencil), the Board section of the library has plain text T, lines/arrows (weight, dash, label), links (double-click opens them), a live Info card mirroring the Shot info panel, and a Production note template for crew, address and contacts. Notes take a title plus bold / italic / size styling. Anything can be locked (selection bar) so set dressing stays put, sent to front / back, and every label chip can be dragged to a better spot once its object is selected. On touch screens, pinch to zoom; the ☰ and ☷ buttons open the panels. Recce & mood keeps reference images per shot — drop image files on the canvas or hit → Board to pin one as a resizable still on the plan. Any pinned image can become an underlay (its selection bar): it drops behind the walls at half opacity — perfect for tracing a location from a satellite screenshot. Take a Google Maps capture with north up, drop it in, toggle Underlay, scale it against the ruler, trace the building and roads, and rotate the sun's N handle to match. Sticky notes N go anywhere; double-click to type.

Export

The Export button adapts to where you are: on the designer it offers the scene as PNG (whole or cropped area) or a PDF with one page per scene — plan, shot list, script and stills. On the boards it exports that board as PNG or a one-page PDF.
