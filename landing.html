How it works

A floor plan in a minute, to scale.

One unit is one centimetre. Drag a room, tap the walls for doors and windows, drop furniture with real sizes, then bring in the crew. Every object is a thing the film needs, not a drawing.

Scene 1 · Atelier — walls
  1. 1Draw the room

    Drag a rectangle for four walls at once, or wall by wall. Snaps to 90° and to wall ends; the middle handle bows a wall into a curve.

  2. 2Doors and windows

    Tap a wall to place a door, a window or an opening, then slide it along. Select it for width and the door swing.

  3. 3Dress the set

    Tables, sofas, kitchens, beds, cars, trees — from the library, all at their real size. Or trace a satellite capture as an underlay.

  4. 4Cast, cameras, lens, light

    Cast with names, cameras with sensor and lens — pick 24, 35 or 50 mm and the field of view follows — a dolly on its track, LED, HMI and bounce, and the sun for the time of day.

  5. 5Play the blocking

    Give an actor a path and a camera a move: the dolly rides its track while Anna crosses the room. What you see here is the plan as it plays — shot list and call sheet follow from it.

  6. Or skip the drawing: scan it

    Floor Scanner on your iPhone walks the room with LiDAR or measures it with the camera — walls, doors, windows and furniture land in your room library, ready to insert in any scene. About Floor Scanner →

2nd floor · Shot designer

See the shot before you light it.

A camera in Floorboard is a real camera: pick the lens and the field of view follows, set framing, support and shot name. Cast has names, lights have a throw.

  • Lens presets 10–135 mm on the sensor you shoot on — full frame, Super 35, Alexa 35, Mini LF, APS-C, Micro 4/3 and more; purple handles set field of view and reach in one drag.
  • Movement: keyframes for cast and cameras, dolly track, jib and technocrane a camera can ride. Press play.
  • Setups A / B for lighting or blocking variants of the same scene.
  • Sun and north for the time of day; drop a still on a camera as its viewfinder frame.
  • Made for the iPad and the pencil: palm rejection, big targets, pinch to zoom.

1st floor · Script

The script breaks itself down.

Import a screenplay (text, Fountain, PDF) or an AV table from Excel. Every scene heading, or every SC number, becomes a scene with its own floor plan. Change the script later and the scenes follow; nothing you planned is thrown away.

  • AV script card: SC · time that adds up · seconds · stills · audio · video · regie notes, plus your own columns.
  • Drop images on a row for stills; storyboard rows chain frames per scene.
  • Export the script or AV script as PDF or Word.
Script & AV script

3rd floor · Shot list

Shooting order, with the clock running.

Every camera you placed is a shot. Pull them onto a day card, drag them into shooting order and add breaks, build time and company moves. Start times count from the call, so the wrap is always in view.

  • Camera, lens and move come from the Shot designer — still editable, one tap to sync.
  • Video, audio and regie notes per shot, stills if you want them.
  • Move a shot to another day with one tap; PDF per day or for the whole shoot.

5th floor · Production

The call sheet writes itself.

Crew, cast and client live in one registry with real roles. The day header holds calls and locations, the prop list what travels. The call sheet composes all of it, live, with clickable phone numbers and addresses.

  • Documents in one place: scripts, AV scripts, prop and gear lists, shot list, budget — PDF, Word, CSV.
  • Your house style on every PDF: accent colour, logo, footer.
  • Mail the call sheet to crew, cast or client; share read-only links with comment pins; invite a co-editor.
Production board

4th floor · Budget

A budget laid out like a quote.

Pre-production, production, post and variable costs, each with as many lines as the job needs. Standard lines get you started; crew and cast come from the registry with the number of shoot days already filled in.

  • Target per phase, contingency and VAT; the cards show where you stand.
  • Gear lines from what is actually on the scene boards.
  • Export as a quote-style PDF or CSV.

Ground floor · Mood

Start with the feeling.

A free board for references, palettes and thoughts: images, animated GIFs, video, audio, PDFs, links, sticky notes and columns. Group into sub-boards. Everything you drop here can travel to a camera as a frame later.
